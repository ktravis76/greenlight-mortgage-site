Zero down around Longview: the USDA map is bigger than you think

One of exactly two zero-down loans in America does not require military service. It requires the right address — and East Texas has a lot of them.

Published 5 August 2026 · Greenlight Mortgage, Longview TX

The most overlooked program in the region

USDA rural development loans finance eligible homes with zero down payment. Not low. Zero. And the word “rural” does a lot of quiet damage here, because people picture forty acres and a tractor and rule themselves out from the couch.

The map is drawn address by address, not by town or county — and communities just outside Longview qualify constantly. Gilmer, Hallsville, White Oak, Diana, Ore City, Jefferson — homes around all of them regularly fit.

Two things have to line up

First, the property address has to sit inside the current USDA map. Second, your household income has to fit the limit for the area and your family size. Both are checkable in minutes, and both change periodically — which is why the answer is checking, not assuming.

If either one misses, USDA is off the table and we will tell you what does fit instead. If both line up, the down payment conversation is over before it starts.

What zero down does and does not mean

It means the purchase price is fully financed. It does not mean the transaction is free — closing costs still exist, though there are legitimate ways to handle them, including seller contributions.

What it changes is the timeline. The five years you were going to spend saving a down payment can become this year, in a house, building your own equity instead of a landlord's.
